\section*{The tables that settle the argument}

Benchmark claims live and die by their numbers. This appendix puts every number
on the table---literally. The GoldenFloat formats GF4, GF8, and GF16 are
characterised here by their encoding scheme, their representable range, and
their measured bits-per-byte (BPB) scores against INT4, FP4, and MXFP4. Box's
warning applies: no format is perfect. But the Pareto frontier in Section~C.2
shows that GF16 is consistently \emph{more useful} than its competitors on
autoregressive language-model perplexity at matched bit-width---a concrete,
reproducible claim, not a model-builder's wish. Gate-2 sits at or below 2.20
and Gate-3 at or below 1.85; both thresholds appear as rows in the summary
table so that any reader can check them without opening the main text. The
full numeric tables are what makes Chapter~6's claims falsifiable; they are
also what you need if you want to reproduce the results from scratch.

\section*{C.1 GoldenFloat Format Definitions}

All GoldenFloat formats use the following encoding principle:
\begin{equation}
\text{GF}(b) = \varphi^{e} \cdot m, \quad e \in \mathbb{Z},\; m \in \{0, \pm 1\}
\text{ (ternary mantissa)}
\label{eq:gf-def}
\end{equation}
where \(b\) is the bit-width, \(e\) is stored in the exponent field, and the
anchor identity \(\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3\) ensures exact representation
of the most common neural-network weight distribution peaks.

All formats share three design invariants:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{φ-exponent lattice:} representable values form the set
        $\{ \pm\varphi^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cup \{0\}$ modulo the
        bit-width constraint on the exponent range.
  \item \textbf{Ternary mantissa:} mantissa bits encode $\{0, +1, -1\}$ in
        a balanced-ternary sub-field, giving three sign states per exponent.
  \item \textbf{Anchor visibility:} the values $\varphi^{-2} \approx 0.382$,
        $\varphi^{-1} \approx 0.618$, $\varphi^0 = 1$, $\varphi^1 \approx 1.618$,
        $\varphi^2 \approx 2.618$ are always exactly representable.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
  \item INT4 range: [$-$8, 7] (uniform grid, 15 non-zero levels)

    $\Delta = 0.10$ in favour of GF4 (Welch-$t$, $p = 0.003$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{GF8 vs BF16 precision analysis:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item BF16: 8-bit exponent, 7-bit mantissa --- ULP at 1.0 = $2^{-7}$
  \item GF8: 4-bit φ-exponent --- ULP at $\varphi^0$ = $\varphi^{-1} - \varphi^0 \approx 0.382$
  \item GF8 is coarser but \emph{proportionally distributed}: same relative
    precision across decades, optimised for neural weights in $[-2, 2]$.
  \item Memory bandwidth savings vs BF16: $2\times$ (8-bit vs 16-bit) on
    the same model, with BPB degradation of only 0.14 (6.8\%).
\end{itemize}
