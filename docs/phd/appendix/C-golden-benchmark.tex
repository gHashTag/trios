\section*{C.1 GoldenFloat Format Definitions}

All GoldenFloat formats use the following encoding principle:
\begin{equation}
\text{GF}(b) = \varphi^{e} \cdot m, \quad e \in \mathbb{Z},\; m \in \{0, \pm 1\}
\text{ (ternary mantissa)}
\label{eq:gf-def}
\end{equation}
where \(b\) is the bit-width, \(e\) is stored in the exponent field, and the
anchor identity \(\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3\) ensures exact representation
of the most common neural-network weight distribution peaks.

\textbf{GF4 vs INT4 comparison:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item INT4 range: [−8, 7] (uniform grid, 15 non-zero levels)
  \item GF4 range: [−6.854, 6.854] (φ-geometric grid, 14 non-zero levels)
  \item GF4 advantage: values cluster near weight distribution peak at
    $|w| \approx 0.618$ (matches LLM weight histograms; see Ch.~\ref{ch:6},
    Fig.~6.2).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{GF8 vs BF16 precision:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item BF16: 8-bit exponent, 7-bit mantissa — ULP at 1.0 = $2^{-7}$
  \item GF8: 4-bit φ-exponent — ULP at $\varphi^0$ = $\varphi^{-1} - \varphi^0 \approx 0.382$
  \item GF8 is coarser but \emph{proportionally distributed}: same relative
    precision across decades, optimised for neural weights in $[-2, 2]$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{C.5 Range × Precision Pareto Frontier}

The Pareto frontier is defined over the two-objective space:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Objective 1 (Range):} $\log_{\varphi}$(max representable value)
  \item \textbf{Objective 2 (Precision):} BPB on sealed evaluation set
\end{itemize}

GF formats dominate INT formats of equal bit-width on Objective~2 at the cost
of slightly reduced Objective~1 range. Full Pareto tables are in
Chapter~\ref{ch:9} (Table~9.2) and archived in Zenodo (App.~H).
