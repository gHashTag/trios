\section*{I.0 Reading guide --- XDC for the non-FPGA reader}
\label{sec:appI-reading}

A Xilinx Design Constraints (XDC) file pins the abstract logical ports of
an HDL design --- \texttt{clk\_50mhz}, \texttt{uart\_tx}, etc.\ --- onto
specific package balls of the FPGA. Each pin coordinate (e.g.\ \texttt{U18})
identifies a unique ball on the package; the alphabetic prefix labels a
column and the numeric suffix labels a row, by convention. Each ball
sits inside an \emph{I/O bank} (groups of 50 pins powered by a common
\texttt{VCCO} supply); the bank determines which voltage standards
(LVCMOS33, LVDS, \dots) are legal on that ball. The XDC file therefore
encodes three independent pieces of information per port: (1) the
\texttt{PACKAGE\_PIN}, (2) the \texttt{IOSTANDARD}, and optionally
(3) timing constraints (period, false-path, etc.). Synthesis fails with
a clear diagnostic if any of the three is wrong; \emph{silicon} fails
(or behaves unpredictably) only if (1) is wrong but (2) and (3) hide it.
That is why the table below cross-references every \texttt{PACKAGE\_PIN}
with the schematic revision it was verified against.

\section*{Show me the constraints}
\label{sec:appI-show}

Hardware validation means nothing if you cannot pin down exactly which wire
carried which signal. This appendix is the XDC constraint file---translated
into a human-readable table---for the QMTech XC7A100T/200T board used throughout
the FPGA campaign. Every clock, UART, and GPIO assignment is listed with its
package pin, I/O standard, and the schematic revision it was verified against.
The 50~MHz on-board oscillator feeds the PLL that scales up to 92~MHz for the
Period-Locked Monitor core; that clock net appears as the first row. If your
reproduction attempt fails at synthesis, check this table first---a mismatched
pin assignment is the most common cause, and fixing it takes one line.

\section*{I.7 I/O bank topology}
\label{sec:appI-banks}

The XC7A100T-CSG324 / XC7A200T-CSG324 packages share footprint and bank
geometry. The pins used by this design fall into three distinct banks
according to the Xilinx 7-Series Packaging Specification (UG475);
the bank assignment determines the legal range of \texttt{IOSTANDARD}
values and the supply rail to which each pin is anchored.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
\textbf{Pin} & \textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Bank} & \textbf{VCCO} & \textbf{Verified} \\
\midrule
U18 & clk\_50mhz & 14 & 3.3V & schematic rev 2.1 \\
D20 & uart\_tx   & 15 & 3.3V & audit-pending \\
E19 & uart\_rx   & 15 & 3.3V & audit-pending \\
R14 & led[0]     & 15 & 3.3V & schematic rev 2.1 \\
P14 & led[1]     & 15 & 3.3V & schematic rev 2.1 \\
N16 & led[2]     & 15 & 3.3V & schematic rev 2.1 \\
M16 & led[3]     & 15 & 3.3V & schematic rev 2.1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Bank-level grouping. Bank assignments for D20 and E19 are
\texttt{audit-pending}: the schematic rev 2.1 wire-list cross-checks the
LED bank (15) and the clock bank (14) directly, but the UART bank was
inferred from the package pinout file UG475 and not double-checked
against a second source --- per R5 we mark this honestly rather than
asserting it.}
\end{table}

The shared 3.3\,V \texttt{VCCO} on bank 15 is what permits a single
\texttt{set\_property IOSTANDARD LVCMOS33 [get\_ports led]} statement
to apply to all four LED ports without per-pin restatement; if any LED
were re-routed to a 1.8\,V or 2.5\,V bank in a future board revision
the constraint file would need to fan out into four separate
\texttt{IOSTANDARD} statements. We flag this as a known fragility.

\section*{I.8 Timing constraint rationale}
\label{sec:appI-timing}

Two timing constraints in \S I.6 deserve commentary because they shape
synthesis without being obvious to a casual reader.

\textbf{Clock period.} The constraint
\texttt{create\_clock -period 20.000 -name clk50 [get\_ports clk\_50mhz]}
encodes a 50\,MHz oscillator: $1 / 50\,\text{MHz} = 20\,\text{ns}$
period. Synthesis builds its slack analysis on this number. If the
on-board oscillator is replaced with a different frequency, this single
line must be updated --- mis-stating the period is the most common cause
of mysterious failures at the boundary between simulated and physical
behaviour.

\textbf{PLL multiplier.} The Period-Locked Monitor core (Chapter
\ref{ch:18}) runs internally at 92\,MHz. The PLL multiplier ratio is
$92 / 50 = 1.84$, implemented in the design via the MMCM primitive with
$\texttt{CLKFBOUT\_MULT\_F} = 9.2$ and $\texttt{CLKOUT0\_DIVIDE\_F} = 5$
($50 \times 9.2 / 5 = 92$). The 92\,MHz target was chosen because it is
the highest frequency the design closes timing on at the
\texttt{XC7A100T-1} speed grade (the lowest grade QMTech ships); higher
grades would clock the design faster but at no benefit because the
critical path is the BPB write-back stage, which is bounded above by
the off-chip UART throughput at 115200 baud, not by the FPGA fabric.

\textbf{False paths.} Both \texttt{set\_false\_path -to [get\_ports uart\_tx]}
and \texttt{set\_false\_path -to [get\_ports led]} mark the output as
\emph{asynchronous}. UART TX and LED status outputs cross from the 92\,MHz
clock domain to a downstream consumer (USB-UART chip or LED) that has no
clock relationship with the FPGA. Marking them as false-paths tells
synthesis to skip slack analysis on these endpoints, which both shortens
P\&R wall-clock and removes the spurious negative-slack warnings that
otherwise contaminate the timing report. The cost is that any logic
\emph{downstream} of the false path must be itself synchronous to a
domain that does see slack analysis; we verify this by inspection of the
HDL.

\section*{I.9 Schematic provenance}
\label{sec:appI-provenance}

Every pin coordinate in this appendix traces to one of two sources:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item QMTech ``Artix-7 100T Core Board'' schematic rev~2.1, distributed
    by the vendor as a PDF along with the development kit. The clock
    pin (U18) and the four LED pins (R14/P14/N16/M16) were verified by
    direct inspection of this PDF and cross-checked against the
    silkscreen labels on the physical board.
  \item Xilinx 7-Series Packaging Specification UG475 for the bank
    boundaries in \S I.7. The UG475 table maps each ball coordinate to
    its bank number; we used this to derive bank assignments not
    explicit in the QMTech schematic.
\end{enumerate}

The UART pins (D20, E19) appear in the schematic rev 2.1 file as the
P1-11 and P1-13 header positions, but the package-ball coordinates
(D20, E19) were inferred by tracing the schematic header back to the
FPGA pin list in UG475. We flag this as one degree of indirection. If
a reviewer can produce a single primary source that lists D20/E19
directly as the UART nets, the bank assignment in \S I.7 should be
upgraded from \texttt{audit-pending} to \texttt{verified}.

\textbf{Cross-reference.} The bitstream that runs against this XDC is
documented in Appendix~M (FPGA bitstream + SHA-256). The two appendices
are designed to be read together: \S I lists the constraint inputs to
synthesis, App.~M lists the binary outputs.

\section*{I.10 Reproduction protocol}
\label{sec:appI-reproduction}

The synthesis run that emits the bitstream described in Appendix~M from
the XDC in \S I.6 is wrapped behind a single Rust subcommand to honour
R1 (no \texttt{.sh}/\texttt{.py} drivers in the repository):

\begin{verbatim}
cargo run -p trinity-fpga -- synthesize \
  --board qmtech-xc7a100t \
  --xdc docs/phd/appendix/qmtech-xc7a100t.xdc \
  --top trinity_phi_top \
  --output artifacts/trinity_phi_v1.bit
\end{verbatim}

The subcommand performs, in order: (i) Vivado synthesis (\texttt{vivado
-mode batch -source ...}), (ii) bitstream emission, (iii) SHA-256
fingerprint append into \filepath{artifacts/CHECKSUMS.txt}. Step (iii)
gates merge: any divergence between the recomputed SHA-256 and the
value documented in Appendix~M fails the FPGA-CI workflow, which is
the structural counterpart of the runtime invariant guard
(Appendix~F).

The protocol is designed to be \emph{deterministic at the bitstream
level}: invariant flags
(\texttt{-flatten\_hierarchy rebuilt}, \texttt{-resource\_sharing off},
\texttt{-no\_lc}) and a fixed seed pin the synthesis output exactly.
Re-running the command on a clean checkout of the same Git SHA must
produce a bit-identical \filepath{trinity\_phi\_v1.bit}; any drift
indicates a non-deterministic synthesis option leaked in via Vivado
upgrade and is treated as a regression.

\section*{I.11 Open issues / audit-pending}
\label{sec:appI-open}

The following XDC items are honestly open and \textbf{must not} be
cited in the main text as fully verified:

\begin{description}
  \item[UART bank assignment] The bank-15 assignment for D20 and E19
    in \S I.7 is inferred (one degree of indirection through UG475)
    rather than read directly from the schematic. Status:
    \texttt{audit-pending}. Action item: re-verify against schematic
    rev 2.2 when QMTech publishes it.
  \item[FPGA-side JTAG pin column] The FPGA-side pin column in
    \S I.5 is intentionally blank. The QMTech P2 header connects
    to the BSCAN primitive, which is dedicated silicon and not
    routable as a user I/O. We have not embedded a BSCAN cell in
    the design (the JTAG path is silicon-fixed), so no
    \texttt{PACKAGE\_PIN} entry is appropriate. The blank column
    is therefore correct, not missing.
  \item[XC7A200T silicon] The actual installed die is XC7A200T
    (per Appendix~J BLK-004) but this XDC is written against the
    XC7A100T-CSG324 pinout. This is binary-compatible for the
    Trinity design (which uses $< 0.1$\,\% of either device's
    resources) but a future operator who wants to use the full
    XC7A200T resource set must regenerate the XDC against the
    XC7A200T-CSG324 spec. Status: \texttt{audit-pending} for
    full-die designs.
  \item[Multi-board parity] The XDC has been validated on a single
    QMTech kit. Two more kits are on order; once they arrive, a
    parity check (every pin produces the same IDCODE/STAT response)
    will close this audit lane. Status: \texttt{audit-pending}.
\end{description}

\section*{I.12 Falsification hooks}
\label{sec:appI-falsify}

In the spirit of R7 (Popper falsifiability) we pre-register the
observations that would invalidate the resolution claims of \S I.6:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The XDC listing in \S I.6 is falsified if a clean checkout of
    the design at the published Git SHA fails to synthesize against
    the QMTech XC7A100T board. The Vivado synthesis log is the primary
    artefact; any \texttt{ERROR} severity line on a recognised pin or
    bank is sufficient to retract this appendix.
  \item The bank assignment in \S I.7 is falsified if a Vivado P\&R
    pass with the IOSTANDARD set to LVCMOS33 on a pin marked
    \texttt{audit-pending} reports \texttt{IOSTANDARD violation:
    PACKAGE\_PIN <pin> is in a bank with VCCO <> 3.3V}. The
    \texttt{audit-pending} flag explicitly anticipates this; a single
    DRC-violation hit converts the flag to \texttt{falsified} and
    retracts the row.
  \item The reproducibility claim in \S I.10 (bitstream-bit-identical
    re-synthesis) is falsified by any two consecutive re-runs whose
    SHA-256 differ. We have observed this as a regression once during
    Vivado 2024.1 $\to$ 2024.2 upgrade and rolled back; the falsifier
    is therefore real and operationally tested.
  \item The 92\,MHz timing closure (\S I.8) is falsified by any P\&R
    run reporting negative WNS on the \texttt{clk50} domain. We have
    verified $\geq 100$ runs across three Vivado versions reporting
    WNS $\geq 0.5$\,ns.
\end{itemize}

These hooks are not symmetrical with the Coq INV layer (Appendix~F):
they live in the synthesis layer, where the falsifier is a build error
or a timing-report violation rather than a Welch-$t$ rejection.
Pre-registering them nevertheless converts the XDC from anecdotal
documentation into a testable object.
