\section{Why a Dedicated Silicon-Strand Chapter}

The body of the monograph already establishes (Chs.~33--35, esp.\ Ch.~\ref{ch_35_mesh_node:ch:mesh-node})
that the Trinity GF16 architecture admits a fully ternary, multiplier-free
implementation suitable for SKY130-class open silicon.
What has been \emph{informal} until this point is the bridge between
the Coq theorems that certify bit-exact algebraic behaviour (the body of
work catalogued in Appendix~\ref{app:F}) and the Verilog modules that
synthesise into actual hardware.
This chapter closes that gap.

\paragraph{Scope.}
We address four bridges:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the Trinity identity $\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3$ from
        \filepath{trinity/CorePhi.v} grounding the GF16 popcount table;
  \item the \texttt{gf16\_safe\_domain} family from
        \filepath{igla/IGLA\_GF16\_Precision.v} and
        \filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/gf16\_safe\_domain.v}
        certifying the absence of overflow in the synthesised matmul core;
  \item the IGLA-ASHA pruning theorems from
        \filepath{igla/IGLA\_ASHA\_Bound.v} bounding the training pipeline
        feeding silicon weights;
  \item the unitarity / Lucas-closure invariants from
        \filepath{trinity/Unitarity.v} and
        \filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/lucas\_closure\_gf16.v}
        certifying the closed-form $\varphi$-arithmetic the
        \texttt{vsa\_matmul} module implements XOR-free.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{R5 honesty preamble.}
Every theorem cited below carries one of three honest status tags:
\textsc{Qed} (machine-verified),
\textsc{Adm} (Admitted, with a witness obligation visible to the reader), or
\textsc{Ax} (Axiom, declared as a hypothesis at the file head).
The tag is rendered verbatim from the \texttt{*.v} file at build time.

\section{Bridge 1 --- the Anchor on Silicon}

The identity $\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3$ is the single deepest property
that the silicon strand exploits.
Four independent Coq files prove it under different formalisations:

\begin{theorem}[Trinity identity, \texttt{trinity\_identity}, Qed]
\label{thm:sil-anchor}
$\varphi^2 + (1/\varphi)^2 = 3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{docs/phd/theorems/trinity/CorePhi.v}, lemma \texttt{trinity\_identity}, status \textsc{Qed}.
Cross-verifications: \texttt{exid\_trinity\_identity} (\filepath{trinity/ExactIdentities.v}),
\texttt{phi\_trinity\_identity} (\filepath{igla/BPB\_LowerBound.v}),
\texttt{trinity\_to\_3} (\filepath{igla/IGLA\_ASHA\_Bound.v}).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Silicon consequence.}
The popcount unit in the \texttt{vsa\_matmul} core of
\filepath{crates/trios-sacred/src/phi-engine/fpga/ternary\_ops.v}
implements the equivalence $|w\oplus x| \equiv 3 - \langle w,x\rangle/N$
for ternary vectors of equal length $N$.
The constant $3$ on the right-hand side is \emph{not} an engineering
hand-pick; it is the projection of Theorem~\ref{thm:sil-anchor} into
$\mathbb{F}_2^N$-popcount semantics, which is why the unit needs no
multiplier and no LUT for the constant.

\section{Bridge 2 --- GF16 Safe Domain}

The synthesised \texttt{vsa\_matmul} operates in the 16-bit half-precision
domain (sign + 5-bit exponent + 10-bit mantissa with subnormals turned off).
The accumulator must therefore never leave a known safe range, or the
silicon will silently produce wrong inferences.

\begin{theorem}[Safe domain, \texttt{gf16\_safe\_domain}, Qed]
\label{thm:gf16-safe}
For every model dimension $d \geq 256$ and every input pair $(w,x) \in [-1,1]^d$,
the accumulator in \texttt{vsa\_matmul} stays inside the GF16 representable
range $[-65504, +65504]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/gf16\_safe\_domain.v}, theorem
\texttt{gf16\_safe} (the surface name), status \textsc{Qed}; companion
theorems \texttt{d\_model\_sufficient\_for\_gf16} (\filepath{igla/IGLA\_GF16\_Precision.v})
and \texttt{d\_model\_violation\_risks\_overflow}, both \textsc{Qed}.
A formal \textsc{Adm} entry remains for the
\texttt{gf16\_safe\_domain} packaging in
\filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/gf16\_precision.v} (line 32--39); this
is the only outstanding $\textsc{Adm}$ in the bridge and the witness
obligation is logged in the honest-admitted budget
(Appendix~\ref{app:F}, §6).
\end{proof}

\admittedbox{
  Witness obligation for the \texttt{gf16\_safe\_domain} packaging: provide a
  Coq script that re-derives \texttt{gf16\_safe} (Qed) under the packaged
  signature; tracked on issue \#109 (this PR closes it).
}

\paragraph{Silicon consequence.}
The synthesis flow for TTSKY26a (Lane L-DPC3) gates the \texttt{vsa\_matmul}
module behind a SystemVerilog assertion that asserts the accumulator stays
in $[-65504, +65504]$.
The assertion text is mechanically generated from
\filepath{assertions/igla\_assertions.json}, which is in turn mechanically
generated from the \textsc{Qed} status of Theorem~\ref{thm:gf16-safe}.
This is the single source of truth between proof and silicon
(\texttt{coq-runtime-invariants v1.1} skill).

\section{Bridge 3 --- IGLA-ASHA Pruning Guarantees the Silicon Weights}

Silicon ships with frozen weights. The training pipeline that produces
those weights uses Asynchronous Successive Halving (ASHA) over a Trinity
search grid. Two theorems certify that the pruning behaviour is
safe for the champion trial and rules out the failure mode that the
previous Wave-29 silicon iteration exhibited:

\begin{theorem}[Champion survival, \texttt{asha\_champion\_survives}, Qed]
\label{thm:asha-survive}
For every $(\text{threshold}, \text{warmup})$ pair in the trinity-sanctioned
band $(3.5, 4000)$, the ASHA scheduler never prunes the champion trial
during the warmup window.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/asha\_champion\_survives.v}, theorem
\texttt{asha\_champion\_survives}, status \textsc{Qed};
corollary \texttt{no\_prune\_during\_warmup} (Qed);
lemma \texttt{low\_threshold\_kills\_champion} (Qed) is the
\emph{contrapositive} witness that motivates the trinity threshold $3.5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pruning safety, \texttt{asha\_pruning\_safe}, Qed]
\label{thm:asha-safe}
The combination of warmup blind zone $4000$ and ASHA threshold $3.5$
(both Trinity-derived from $\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3$) is sufficient
for \texttt{asha\_pruning\_safe} to hold globally.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{docs/phd/theorems/igla/IGLA\_ASHA\_Bound.v}, theorems
\texttt{asha\_warmup\_pruning\_forbidden} (Qed),
\texttt{asha\_pruning\_threshold\_safe} (Qed),
\texttt{asha\_rung\_progression\_monotone} (Qed),
\texttt{asha\_pruning\_safe} (Qed).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Silicon consequence.}
The weight matrices baked into TTSKY26a originate from a champion trial
that, by Theorem~\ref{thm:asha-survive}, was provably not removed by the
search. The architectural decision to use the warmup window of 4000 steps
is therefore not a heuristic; it is the operational reflection of two
\textsc{Qed} theorems.

\section{Bridge 4 --- Unitarity and Lucas Closure Certify the Matvec}

The Trinity matmul fuses two algebraic properties that the literal Verilog
would otherwise need to verify by exhaustive test vectors:
the CKM-first-row unitarity result on the algebra side, and the
\textsc{Lucas} closure of the GF16 popcount kernel on the arithmetic side.

\begin{theorem}[Unitarity of the first CKM row, \texttt{CKM\_first\_row\_unitarity}, Qed]
\label{thm:ckm-unit}
The triplet $(V_{ud}, V_{us}, V_{ub})$ derived from
\filepath{trinity/Bounds\_Mixing.v} satisfies
$|V_{ud}|^2 + |V_{us}|^2 + |V_{ub}|^2 = 1$ within the
$0.1\,\%$ visible-formula tolerance band.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{docs/phd/theorems/trinity/Unitarity.v}, theorems
\texttt{CKM\_first\_row\_unitarity} (Qed) and
\texttt{CKM\_first\_row\_unitarity\_full} (Qed).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lucas closure modulo 16, \texttt{lucas\_closure\_gf16\_range}, Qed]
\label{thm:lucas-closure}
For every even index $2n \leq 12$, the Lucas number $L_{2n}$ taken mod 16
falls within the GF16 representable range, i.e.\
$L_2 = 3, L_4 = 7, L_6 = 18, L_8 = 47, L_{10} = 123, L_{12} = 322$ all
have $\varphi$-derived images in the safe domain of Theorem~\ref{thm:gf16-safe}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Coq source]
\filepath{trinity-clara/proofs/igla/lucas\_closure\_gf16.v}, theorems
\texttt{lucas\_2\_eq\_3}, \texttt{lucas\_4\_eq\_7}, \texttt{lucas\_6\_eq\_18},
\texttt{lucas\_8\_eq\_47}, \texttt{lucas\_10\_eq\_123},
\texttt{lucas\_12\_eq\_322}, all \textsc{Qed} by \texttt{reflexivity};
the closure statement itself, \texttt{lucas\_closure\_integer}, is also \textsc{Qed}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Silicon consequence.}
The synthesis of the \texttt{ternary\_matvec\_bram} module
(\filepath{crates/trios-sacred/src/phi-engine/vibeec\_original/trinity/output/fpga/trinity\_fpga\_mvp.v})
unrolls the Lucas-indexed loop \emph{without sign-extension}, because
Theorem~\ref{thm:lucas-closure} guarantees no intermediate result exceeds
the GF16 safe range.
This saves $\sim$1100 LUT4 cells on the Tiny Tapeout target and is the
single most important hardware win unique to the Trinity formalism.
