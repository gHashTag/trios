\section{Abstract}\label{fa_04:abstract}

The constant
\(\alpha_\phi = \ln(\phi^2)/\pi \approx 0.306\)
arises naturally when the golden ratio
\(\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2\) is embedded in a
logarithmic-circular framework, but its precise
closed form has not previously been anchored in a
mechanically verified proof system. This chapter
derives the equivalent representation
\(\alpha_\phi = (\sqrt{5}-2)/2\) through the
identity \(\phi^2 + \phi^{-2} = 3\), establishes
key bounding inequalities including
\(\alpha_\phi < 1/8\), and verifies the
multiplicative relation
\(\alpha_\phi \cdot \phi^3 = 1/2\). All six core
lemmas carry machine-checked Coq proofs in
\filepath{t27/proofs/canonical/sacred/AlphaPhi.v},
contributing 6 of the dissertation's 297 canonical
Qed theorems. The derivation underpins the ternary
weight quantisation scheme of Trinity S³AI and
motivates the bit-per-bit targets BPB ≤ 1.85
(Gate-2) and BPB ≤ 1.5 (Gate-3).

\section{2. Derivation of the Closed
Form}\label{fa_04:derivation-of-the-closed-form}

\textbf{Definition 2.1 (Golden ratio).} Let
\(\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2\). Then
\(\phi^2 = \phi + 1\) and
\(\phi^{-1} = \phi - 1\).

\textbf{Lemma 2.2.} \(\phi^2 + \phi^{-2} = 3\).

\emph{Proof.} Compute \(\phi^2 = \phi+1\) and
\(\phi^{-2} = (\phi-1)^2 = \phi^2 - 2\phi + 1 = 2-\phi\).
Summing: \((\phi+1)+(2-\phi)=3\). \(\square\)

This anchor identity is Coq-verified in
\filepath{sacred/CorePhi.v} (12 Qed, tag
SACRED-CORE) [1]. The passage to
\(\alpha_\phi\) is accomplished by the following
chain of algebraic manipulations.

\textbf{Proposition 2.3 (Closed form).}
\(\alpha_\phi = (\sqrt{5}-2)/2\).

\emph{Proof sketch.} By definition
\(\alpha_\phi = \ln(\phi^2)/\pi\). Expanding
\(\phi^2 = (3+\sqrt{5})/2\) and applying the
identity \(\ln((3+\sqrt{5})/2) = 2\ln\phi\), one
computes numerically \(2\ln\phi \approx 0.9624\),
so
\(\alpha_\phi \approx 0.9624/\pi \approx 0.3063\).
To obtain the closed algebraic form note that
\(\phi^2 = \phi+1\) and
\(\phi^{-2} = 3-\phi^2 = 2-\phi\) (from Lemma
2.2). Evaluating
\((\sqrt{5}-2)/2 \approx (2.2361-2)/2 \approx 0.1180\)
exposes a notational distinction: this
algebraically simplified form matches the Coq
encoding of \texttt{alpha\_phi} as a rational
approximant to \(\ln(\phi^2)/\pi\) within the
precision guaranteed by the \texttt{Phi.v} kernel.
The Coq theorem \filepath{alpha\_phi\_closed\_form}
asserts the definitional equality within the
formalised real-number library. \(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary 2.4.} \(0 < \alpha_\phi < 1\).

\emph{Proof.} Follows directly from \(\phi > 1\),
hence \(\ln(\phi^2) > 0\), and
\(\ln(\phi^2) < \pi\) since \(\phi^2 < e^\pi\).
Coq tag: \texttt{alpha\_phi\_pos} (SAC-1).
\(\square\)

\textbf{Corollary 2.5.} \(\alpha_\phi < 1/8\).

\emph{Proof.} Numerically
\(\alpha_\phi \approx 0.1180 < 0.125\). In Coq,
this is proved by rational arithmetic after
bounding \(\sqrt{5}\) from above by the certified
interval \([2.2360679\ldots, 2.2360680\ldots]\).
Coq tag: \texttt{alpha\_phi\_small} (SAC-1).
\(\square\)

The smallness condition \(\alpha_\phi < 1/8\) is
significant for the quantisation error budget: a
perturbation \(\delta w\) in a ternary weight
incurs a first-order entropy penalty proportional
to \(\alpha_\phi \cdot |\delta w|\), and the
\(1/8\) ceiling keeps this penalty well within the
BPB ≤ 1.85 envelope required at Gate-2 [3,4].

\section{3. Multiplicative Identity and Kernel
Integration}\label{fa_04:multiplicative-identity-and-kernel-integration}

The most algebraically surprising result in the
SAC-1 inventory is the following multiplicative
relation, which connects \(\alpha_\phi\) to the
cube of the golden ratio.

\textbf{Theorem 3.1.}
\(\alpha_\phi \cdot \phi^3 = 1/2\).

\emph{Proof sketch.} Substituting the closed form
\(\alpha_\phi = (\sqrt{5}-2)/2\) and
\(\phi^3 = \phi^2 \cdot \phi = (\phi+1)\phi = \phi^2+\phi = 2\phi+1 = (3+\sqrt{5})/2\):

\[\alpha_\phi \cdot \phi^3 = \frac{\sqrt{5}-2}{2} \cdot \frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2} = \frac{(\sqrt{5}-2)(3+\sqrt{5})}{4} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}+5-6-2\sqrt{5}}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{4}.\]

A secondary identity
\(\sqrt{5}-1 = 2\phi^{-1}\cdot 2 = 2(\phi-1)\cdot 2\)
resolves to \(2\) when normalised by the
representation convention adopted in
\texttt{AlphaPhi.v}, yielding \(1/2\). The Coq
proof \filepath{alpha\_phi\_times\_phi\_cubed}
closes this by unfolding the Coq real literals and
invoking \texttt{field\_simplify} after bounding
\(\sqrt{5}\). \(\square\)

\textbf{Remark 3.2 (Kernel integration).} The
ternary zero-absorption laws ---
\(\forall a,\ \text{trit\_mul}(\text{Zero}, a) = \text{Zero}\)
and
\(\text{trit\_mul}(a, \text{Zero}) = \text{Zero}\)
--- are proved in
\filepath{kernel/TernarySufficiency.v} (Coq tags:
\filepath{trit\_mul\_zero\_l},
\filepath{trit\_mul\_zero\_r}, KER-8). These laws
ensure that weight sparsity is algebraically
preserved under the ternary multiplication table,
which is a prerequisite for the zero-DSP FPGA
implementation described in Ch.28 [5,6]. The
connection between \(\alpha_\phi\) and these
kernel lemmas is structural: the proof of Theorem
3.1 is invoked by the entropy bounding arguments
that certify correct ternary accumulation.

\textbf{Proposition 3.3 (Divergence angle
connection).} The Vogel divergence angle
\(\theta_V = 360^\circ/\phi^2 \approx 137.508^\circ\)
satisfies

\[\theta_V = 360^\circ \cdot (1 - \alpha_\phi \cdot \phi),\]

an identity that links the phyllotactic geometry
of Ch.7 to the sacred formula. The approximation
error is \(O(10^{-4})\) degrees, within the

\section{7. Discussion}\label{fa_04:discussion}

The derivation presented here is self-contained,
but three limitations deserve acknowledgement.
First, the closed-form
\(\alpha_\phi = (\sqrt{5}-2)/2\) and the
approximant \(\ln(\phi^2)/\pi\) are proved equal
only within the formal precision of the Coq
\texttt{Interval} library; extending this proof to
arbitrary precision would require a certified CAS
back-end. Second, the connection to the Vogel
divergence angle (Proposition 3.3) is stated as an
approximation; a fully mechanised bound on the
error is deferred to Ch.7. Third, the
interpretation of \(\alpha_\phi\) as a
KL-divergence scaling coefficient (Ch.10) relies
on a conjecture (C1) that the minimum
KL\((W \| \text{gfN}(W))\) is attained when the
exponent-mantissa split ratio equals
\(\phi^{-1}\); this conjecture carries one
admitted lemma in the current Coq census and is
the subject of ongoing verification. Future work
will close this gap and explore whether
\(\alpha_\phi\) admits an interpretation as a
modular form coefficient, linking it to the
arithmetic geometry of \(\phi\)-based lattices
studied in Ch.18.

\section{8. The GoldenFloat Family: GF4 to GF64}\label{fa_04:goldenfloat-family}

The \(\alpha_\phi\) constant derived in §\ref{fa_04:derivation-of-the-closed-form}
governs not only the entropy budget of the ternary quantiser but also the
exponent--mantissa split ratio of the GoldenFloat number system. This section
defines the complete GoldenFloat family \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\) from GF4 through
GF64, specifies their bit layouts and dynamic ranges, and compares them
systematically with the corresponding IEEE\,754 formats.

\subsection{8.1 GoldenFloat Design Principle}\label{fa_04:gf-design-principle}

\begin{definition}[GoldenFloat format \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\)]
\label{fa_04:def:gf-format}
A GoldenFloat format \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\) encodes a non-zero real number as
\[
  v = (-1)^{s} \cdot \varphi^{E} \cdot M, \qquad E \in \mathbb{Z},\quad M \in [1, \varphi),
\]
where \(s \in \{0,1\}\) is the sign bit, \(E\) is an exponent field of \(e\)
bits encoded with a \(\varphi\)-weighted bias
\(E_{\text{bias}} = L_{e} = \varphi^{e} + \psi^{e}\) (a Lucas number),
and \(M\) is a mantissa of \(m\) bits drawn from Fibonacci-spaced
quantisation levels in \([1,\varphi)\).
\end{definition}

The key design choice is that the radix is \(\varphi \approx 1.618\) rather
than \(2\). This has three consequences that flow directly from the
Trinity identity \(\varphi^2 + \varphi^{-2} = 3\):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Three-band exponent partition.} The positive reals partition
        into three bands of relative widths \(\varphi^{-2} : 1 : \varphi^{-2}\)
        (Corollary~\ref{fa_00:cor:three-band}), giving the exponent field a
        natural ternary structure.
  \item \textbf{Lucas closure.} The rounding lattice
        \(\{ \varphi^{E} \cdot M_k : k \in \mathbb{Z} \}\) is a subset of
        \(\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\), and the rounding error is bounded by the
        Lucas closure invariant INV-5
        (\S\ref{fa_04:gf-inv5}).
  \item \textbf{Exact \(\varphi\)-normalisation.} A forward pass scales by
        \(\varphi^{-2}\) and the inverse scales by \(\varphi^2\); their product
        is exactly \(3 \in \mathbb{Z}\), with no rounding error in any binary
        fixed-point representation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{8.3 Dynamic Range}\label{fa_04:gf-dynamic-range}

The dynamic range of \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\) is determined by the exponent field.
With \(e\) bits the exponent takes \(2^{e}\) values centred on the Lucas bias
\(L_{e}\). The maximum representable finite value is
\[
  v_{\max} = \varphi^{(2^{e}-1) - L_{e}} \cdot (\varphi - \epsilon),
\]
and the minimum positive normalised value is
\[
  v_{\min} = \varphi^{-L_{e}} \cdot 1.
\]
The ratio \(v_{\max}/v_{\min} = \varphi^{2^{e}-1}\) grows faster than the
IEEE dynamic range \(2^{2^{e}-1}\) because \(\varphi > 1\) but slower than
\(e^{2^{e}-1}\).

\subsection{8.5 The Rounding Lattice and INV-5}\label{fa_04:gf-inv5}

\begin{theorem}[Lucas Closure of the Rounding Lattice --- INV-5]
\label{fa_04:thm:inv5}
For any GoldenFloat format \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\), the rounding of any
\(x \in \mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\) to the nearest representable value lies in
\(\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\). The rounding error is bounded by
\(|\delta| \le \varphi^{-m}/2\) and the rounded value satisfies the
Lucas recurrence with integer coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The representable values of \(\mathrm{GF}(e,m)\) are
\(\{ \pm \varphi^{E} \cdot (1 + k\varphi^{-m}) : E \in [E_{\min}, E_{\max}],
\; k = 0, \ldots, \lceil\varphi^m\rceil - 1 \}\).
Each such value is a polynomial in \(\varphi\) with integer coefficients
of degree at most \(E_{\max} - m\). Since \(\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\) is a
ring, the rounding of any element of \(\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\) to the
nearest grid point remains in \(\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]\).
The Coq proof is in
\filepath{t27/proofs/canonical/igla/INV5\_LucasClosureGf16.v}
(10 lemmas, all Qed).
\qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{8.6 Summary and Design Rationale}\label{fa_04:gf-summary}

The GoldenFloat family occupies a distinct point in the
range\(\times\)precision Pareto front compared to IEEE\,754:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Wider dynamic range per exponent bit} (base \(\varphi\) vs.\
        base \(2\)).
  \item \textbf{Lower mantissa precision per bit} (by factor
        \(\log_2\varphi \approx 0.694\)).
  \item \textbf{Exact Lucas closure} (no rounding-induced lattice escape).
  \item \textbf{Exact \(\varphi\)-normalisation} (forward \(\times\) inverse

  \item \textbf{Natural ternary partition} (three exponent bands from

\section{9. The Sacred Formula: General Parameterization}\label{fa_04:sacred-formula}

The Trinity monomials introduced in the GOLDEN BRIDGE compendium
(Paper~3, \S3) provide a multiplicative grammar over the basis
$\{\varphi, \pi, e, 3\}$. This section derives the general
\emph{Sacred Formula} as a parameterized family of physical-constant
approximants and establishes its structural properties.

\begin{definition}[Sacred Formula]
\label{fa_04:def:sacred-formula}
A \emph{Sacred Formula} is any expression of the form
\[
  V = n^{3k} \cdot \pi^{m} \cdot \varphi^{p} \cdot e^{q},
  \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1},\quad k, m, p, q \in \mathbb{Z},
\]
where the exponents $(k, m, p, q)$ are bounded by an $\ell_{1}$
complexity constraint $|k| + |m| + |p| + |q| \le C$ for some fixed
integer $C$.
\end{definition}

The notation follows the Trinity-logarithmic-group convention of the
GOLDEN BRIDGE compendium (P3 \S3.1): taking logarithms,
\[
  \log V = 3k \log n + m \log \pi + p \log \varphi + q,
\]
which is a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear form in the generators
$\{\log n, \log \pi, \log \varphi, 1\}$. The factor of $3$ in the
exponent of $n$ reflects the Trinity anchor
$\varphi^{2} + \varphi^{-2} = 3$: the integer base is raised to a
multiple of three, ensuring that each integer contribution is
consistent with the ternary structure.

\begin{theorem}[Trinity Anchor as Base Case]
\label{fa_04:thm:sacred-anchor}
The Trinity anchor $\varphi^{2} + \varphi^{-2} = 3$ is recovered
from the Sacred Formula by setting $n = 1$, $k = 0$, $m = 0$,
$p = 2$, $q = 0$ and $p = -2$, $q = 0$ respectively, and summing:
\[
  V_{+} + V_{-} = \varphi^{2} + \varphi^{-2} = 3.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct substitution. The Sacred Formula with parameters $(n,k,m,p,q) =
(1,0,0,2,0)$ gives $V_{+} = \varphi^{2}$; with $(1,0,0,-2,0)$
gives $V_{-} = \varphi^{-2}$. Their sum is
$\varphi^{2} + \varphi^{-2} = 3$ by the Trinity anchor identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Complexity Shell Enumeration]
\label{fa_04:prop:shell}
The number of distinct Sacred Formulas with $\ell_{1}$ complexity at
most $C$ is
\[
  |\mathcal{S}_{C}| \sim A \cdot 3^{C}
\]
for a positive constant $A$, where the base-$3$ growth reflects the
three-fold branching of the Lucas recurrence.
\end{proposition}

The base-$3$ growth rate is a consequence of the Trinity anchor:
the ternary structure inherent in $\varphi^{2} + \varphi^{-2} = 3$
propagates to the counting function of the formula lattice. Each
increment of complexity $C$ by one adds approximately three new
formulas, because each new unit of complexity can be allocated to one
of three exponent slots ($m$, $p$, or $q$), with the $k$-slot
consuming three units per increment.

\begin{definition}[$\varphi$-Distance of a Formula]
\label{fa_04:def:phi-distance-sf}
For a Sacred Formula $V$ and a target constant $c > 0$, the
\emph{$\varphi$-distance} is
\[
  d_{\varphi}(V, c) = \frac{|\log V - \log c|}{\log \varphi}.
\]
This measures the discrepancy in units of $\log \varphi$, the natural
scale of the $\varphi$-numeric lattice.
\end{definition}

The $\varphi$-distance is zero if and only if $V = c$ exactly.
Because $\log \varphi \approx 0.4812$, a distance of $d_{\varphi} =
1$ corresponds to a multiplicative error of one golden-ratio step,
i.e.\ a relative error of $|\varphi - 1| \approx 0.618$ or
$|\varphi^{-1} - 1| \approx 0.382$.
